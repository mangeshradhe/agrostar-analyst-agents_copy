The pattern matters: the system keeps most of its movement short — 64% of its tonnage stays within a state, and it does nearly half of all the free Intra-FC moves. Manufacturing TOs are inter-state heavy by nature (factories are in Gujarat, Maharashtra and Rajasthan; demand is national) — the lever there is routing to the right first destination, not avoiding the distance.

Gap in our own master data: even combining both constants files, two things have no distance — the Aurangabad↔Pune lane (our single biggest, 372 t this year) and every manufacturing site. Around 31% of paid tonnage moves on routes our transfer engine has no distance for.

June's spike is the bill for April–May's pre-season push: stock destinations couldn't sell came flooding back mid-season. Manual pushes drive the majority of returned weight, and by item count it's even clearer — 73% of round trips start with a manual push. In 34,000 cases the return leg was raised by the system, paying to bring back what a manual order had sent. Pure system flip-flops exist (13,000 items, ~30 t across May–June) and need the cool-off rule too, but they are the smaller half. July looks better on both sides so far.

Timing tells a story: 84% of round trips complete within 30 days, and the largest share starts and reverses inside the same calendar month — a pattern consistent with stock being moved to meet month-end targets and returned after.

Four distinct behaviours hide in there: (1) instant rejections — the soybeans came back in 1–2 days, meaning the destination refused them on arrival (fix: acceptance check); (2) seed-timing misses — maize/paddy bounced in 3–13 days with 200+ days of viability left, i.e., sowing-window roulette (fix: gate seed transfers on sowing/weather signals, not monthly budgets); (3) heavy manure on long paid legs — Parakh + Sanchaar are the wasted-kilometre champions at 20–25 kg per unit (fix: stricter inter-state threshold for heavy low-value CN); (4) same-city channel shuffle — ~39 t bounced between the B2B ('02') and B2C ('01') buildings of the same city: zero freight but real handling churn (fix: decide the channel split before stocking). One hypothesis died: ping-pong is not expiry-driven — bounced stock had 200–1,500 days of shelf life, with a single exception (G-20 Groundnut, ~80 days left, 100% near-expiry — that SKU needs liquidation, not another transfer).

What is causing it — the verdict from the monthly cause map: the dominant pattern in every month except May is a manual push that the system later reverses (M→S). May is the system's one bad month and it is essentially one SKU — Parakh manure, which manual pushed in April (M→S) and the system pushed back out in May (S→M): the two sides ping-ponged the same product at each other across two months. Three out of four inter-state transfer orders weigh less than 200 kg — parcel-sized loads crossing state borders one order at a time. Consolidating each lane into a weekly milk-run is likely the single biggest operational saving in this report, and it needs no algorithm — just a dispatch calendar. CPO — which categories consume the TCI

Crop Nutrition is the freight hog: 1,215 t and 3.2 lakh tonne-km — more TCI than insecticides and herbicides combined — because it's heavy and cheap per kg (25 kg fertilizer bags, manure). Field-crop seeds are second (2.6 lakh t-km) and are seasonal by nature. The CPO question: for heavy, low-value CN lines, is a second regional source or factory-direct routing cheaper than any amount of transfer optimization?

CTO — the algorithm verdict

We tested whether the two recommendation programmes fight each other (budget-balancing pushing out, UF-case pulling back). They don't — that overlap is tiny (1,364 items). The real defect is simpler: the UF-case engine reverses itself — 10,532 items went out on a UF recommendation and came back on another UF recommendation ~17 days later. The engine has no memory of its own recent moves; the fix is a cool-off feature inside the recommendation logic, not a redesign. (For symmetry: the manual world has the same disease — DRR-Fulfil reversed its own moves for 26 t.)

9 · What we should do

Seven actions, in order of money

  1. Consolidate small inter-state shipments. 75% of inter-state TOs weigh under 200 kg. A weekly dispatch calendar per lane turns parcel-sized trips into full loads — the biggest operational saving here, no algorithm needed.
  2. Add a 30-day cool-off rule — including inside the UF engine. Block or force-review any transfer that reverses a route the same product travelled in the past 30 days. Catches ~84% of round-trip waste, would have blunted June's 75-tonne return wave, and cures the UF engine's self-reversal (10,500 items).
  3. Let the destination confirm before we ship. Akola↔Aurangabad is the worst case: the system sent 29 tonnes that were manually shipped back within 6 days. One acceptance check stops it.
  4. Ship factory output to its real destination. Karjan stock waits ~54 days in Aurangabad before moving on; Pune receives factory goods second-hand. Route by final demand — Bagru→Jaipur shows we already know how.
  5. Review manual bulk (BM) pushes. Only 46% sold within 60 days, and the system reversed 30 tonnes of them at our own freight cost. Every reversal is a documented wrong call — use the list to retrain planning.
  6. Book all factory dispatches as Manufacturing TOs (VTO) in WMS. Panvel (544 t) and the smaller Gujarat sites currently ship under ordinary UTO numbers. This report reclassifies them by origin, but the source booking should be fixed so every system and report sees factory output correctly.
  7. Fix the basics. Add the Aurangabad↔Pune lane and all factory sites to the distance constants, make facility codes a dropdown, and never truck stock out of a city when a free Intra-FC move exists (35 tonnes did exactly that).

Fine print

Definitions. TCI = travel distance × weight (tonne-km), computed from the official FC distance constants. Intra-FC = both facilities in the same city (distance constant 0). Inter-City = same state, different cities. Inter-State = across states. System = transfer orders created from CRM recommendations ("System TO"); Manual = orders raised by planners; Manufacturing TO = any transfer originating at a manufacturing site (classified by origin, since Panvel and some Gujarat sites currently book under UTO numbers instead of VTO).

Method. WMS transfer invoices joined to transfer headers and sales invoices, 1 Apr – 13 Jul 2026. Cancelled orders and lines are excluded everywhere except section 7. "Sold within 60 days" tracks barcoded serials only, on cohorts with a full 60-day window (transfers to 14 May); the month-cohort table uses a 30-day window so April–June are comparable. Consumption counts only clean sales — cancelled sales lines (0.9% of the sales table) and returns are excluded. Revenue = pre-GST sales value of the first sale of each transferred barcoded item. Wasted distance uses only routes present in the official constants files.

Caveats. The year is 3.5 months old; recent transfers haven't had time to sell. July figures cover 13 days. Manufacturing-TO stock is excluded from sell-through because it moves onward before reaching farmers; most June–July factory stock is still at hubs, so its dwell numbers will grow.
